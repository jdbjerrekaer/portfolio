## Recruiter takeaway

Yet Another Countdown is here to show solo product craft: finding a small broken market, defining the interaction model, designing the mobile UI, implementing it, and carrying native integrations through to a polished experience.

What this proves:

- I can own UX, UI, implementation, and native-feeling polish end-to-end
- I design mobile products around real interaction constraints, not just screen layouts
- I can make privacy, permissions, widgets, shortcuts, and local-first behavior feel calm

I just wanted a countdown app that worked.

Every option in the App Store told the same story: cluttered interfaces, outdated design patterns, and core features locked behind paywalls that felt out of proportion to what you actually got. Want more than three countdowns? Pay. Want a widget? Pay more. Want it to look decent or support dark mode? Good luck—most hadn't been updated in months, some in years.

Meanwhile, iOS kept shipping new capabilities: modern widget APIs, refined design language, better notification handling. And these apps weren't keeping up. The gap between what a native iOS experience *should* feel like and what these apps delivered kept widening.

So I stopped waiting for someone else to fix it.

## Problem

Countdown apps should be simple, but many App Store options felt cluttered, visually stale, or locked basic features behind subscriptions. The product problem was to make important dates visible without making the app feel heavier than the moments it tracks.

## My role

Solo **Design Engineer** — owned product framing, UX, UI, React/Ionic implementation, Capacitor app setup, calendar import, widgets, shortcuts, dark mode, and release polish.

## Constraints

- Mobile-first interaction had to stay thumb-friendly and readable at a glance
- The app needed to work local-first without accounts or unnecessary data collection
- Native features like widgets, calendar access, notifications, and shortcuts had to feel integrated instead of bolted on

## Key decisions

- Treat future and past dates as one event model so countdown and count-up states stay simple
- Use recurring yearly events instead of duplicating birthdays and anniversaries every year
- Keep monetization optional and light-touch so the core tracking flow stays trustworthy

## Outcome

Yet Another Countdown became a polished local-first countdown app with recurring events, calendar import, dark mode, widgets, and Siri Shortcuts. It is the mobile craft case study: small scope, high finish, and full design-engineering ownership.

The App Store presence gets the same treatment as the product: the screenshot set shown here was designed around the two real audiences — countdown moments and count-up streaks — with each screenshot pairing one benefit headline with a real capture of the app, composed through a scripted, repeatable pipeline rather than one-off mockups.

## What the app is

Yet Another Countdown is a lightweight, iOS-native app for tracking important dates — and it serves two audiences with one model: people counting down to the moments they can't wait for (a concert, a birthday, summer break) and people counting up the milestones they're proud of (days snus-free, days since a big change). It supports both future events (countdown) and past events (count up / "days ago"), with recurring yearly events for anything that repeats. Built with native iOS 26 UI elements, dark mode support, and Siri Shortcuts, it feels right at home on modern iOS whether you're checking at a glance or creating an event hands-free.

## Goals & constraints

- **Mobile-first, thumb-friendly** layout and touch targets
- **Local-first** (no accounts required; everything persists on-device)
- **Recurring events** that "just work" year over year
- **Dark mode parity** that maintains hierarchy and contrast
- **Privacy + consent clarity** when the app needs system permissions
- **Sustainable monetization** without subscriptions or core feature lockups
- **Clear hierarchy**: title + emoji for recognition, time state for urgency, secondary details only when needed
- **Native polish** through haptics, dialogs, and notifications (without breaking on web)

## Key UX decisions

### Count down and count up in the same model

Instead of forcing separate "modes", the app treats past dates as first-class events. The same model that counts down to a concert or a birthday also counts up a streak — days since quitting snus, days since a milestone. Two very different audiences (teens tracking the moments they can't wait for, adults tracking how far they've come) share one simple add flow, with no separate "habit tracker" bolted on.

### Make every countdown feel personal

Each event gets an emoji, a colour, and a shape. The edit sheet suggests emoji contextually from the title, a hue slider tints the icon container, and a long-press on the icon opens a shape popover — circle, hexagon, squircle, heart, or flower. Personalisation is what makes a countdown list feel like *yours*, and it had to be playful without becoming a settings page.

### Recurring yearly events without duplicated items

For birthdays and anniversaries, the event stays a single entity that advances each year. The UI surfaces the *next* occurrence, and the logic calculates repetition count and next date when needed—so the list never explodes into duplicates.

### Live Activities that only show up on the day

A countdown that lands today earns the lock screen. On the day itself the event
becomes a Live Activity: a ticking countdown when it has a real time of day,
"Today" when it is an all-day event, and "28 years today" for a birthday or
anniversary that has already come round.

The interesting part was what iOS would not let me do. I wanted the lock screen
from 24 hours out but the Dynamic Island only on the day — and one
`ActivityConfiguration` renders both surfaces, with no switch to run one without
the other. Rather than fake it with two activities (the Island still appears for
any live activity, so the trick buys nothing), the rule became the mechanism:
start on the day, and the Island stays quiet until then by construction.
ActivityKit also refuses to start an activity from the background, and
push-to-start needs a server this local-first app deliberately does not have —
so the app asks on launch, on every return to the foreground, and whenever the
list changes. Dismiss one and it stays gone for that day; the next occurrence
arms it again.

### Reordering that feels physical

Drag-and-drop is initiated with an intentional long-press and uses careful transform handling to avoid the common "squish/scale" distortion during drag. The tilt is pivoted around the point you actually grabbed: hold a card by its left edge and dragging down drops that edge while the far side lags up, hold it by the right and it mirrors, hold it dead centre and the card stays level — the way a real card behaves in your hand. The lifted preview also reads exactly like the card it came from, down to whether the time is spelled out or abbreviated; the two used to disagree because each measured its own fit.

### Monetization without disrupting flow

The app uses a light-touch, optional model: core features stay free, while a one-time upgrade removes ads. The design goal was to keep the interface calm and transparent. No nagging, no gated features. So the product stays trustworthy.

### Shortcuts for fast capture
Important dates often come up mid-conversation. Siri Shortcuts make creation and quick lookups feel immediate, so users can add or query a countdown without breaking context.

## Design-engineering highlights

- **Native iOS 26 components**: the app uses native iOS UI elements (buttons, lists, modals, date pickers) through Ionic's iOS mode, so the app matches Apple's Human Interface Guidelines rather than approximating them.
- **Live Activities**: a shared `ActivityAttributes` type spans the app and the widget extension; the app composes the localised headline (all eleven languages already live in the web layer) and hands the target date over, so the system ticks the countdown down without a single push.
- **Gestures that own their touch**: the editor's edge-swipe dismissal runs on a non-passive listener and calls `preventDefault`, because a slow drag otherwise hands the touch to native scrolling — whose `touchcancel` snapped the sheet back with the finger still on the screen. The first real movement also drops the keyboard.
- **No number that lies**: the small widget used to render seconds under a "min" label in the final minute, on a timeline that only ticks once a minute. Anything below a minute now shows a dash — a widget should either be right or say nothing.
- **Countdown timing hook**: a dedicated `useCountdown()` updates at 1-second intervals and cleanly handles "today", future, and past states.
- **Yearly recurrence math**: `date-fns` helpers compute next occurrence and repetition count (e.g. "28th birthday") reliably.
- **Notifications across platforms**: on iOS, notifications are scheduled via Capacitor local notifications. On web, we fall back to permission + local persistence with "check on load" behavior (because web notifications can't be scheduled arbitrarily far ahead).
- **Dark mode system**: custom theme tokens map into Ionic's palette so the UI keeps contrast and depth in both modes without duplicating layouts.
- **Siri/App Intents**: native intents for creating, listing, and querying countdowns make the app usable by voice or Shortcuts.
- **Native home screen widgets**: WidgetKit-based widgets in small, medium, and large sizes, with three display styles (Focus, Visual with progress bars, and Classic flip-clock digits). Uses App Groups to keep app and widget on the same data and supports iOS 17+ AppIntents for per-widget event selection. Tapping a widget deep-links directly to that event's edit modal. A triple-event large widget variant shows three countdowns at once.
- **Optional ad-free upgrade**: a one-time purchase with restore support and celebratory feedback, designed to feel respectful rather than transactional.
- **Privacy + consent flows**: AdMob consent/ATT handling is surfaced transparently, with a settings-based reset path to keep user control clear.
- **Calendar import**: native iOS calendar import (via a custom Capacitor plugin) and `.ics` import support. Deduplication normalizes titles across multiple languages and removes age suffixes ("48th Birthday") so yearly events map to a stable identity.
- **Internationalization**: the UI ships with multiple locales so the product can launch beyond English from day one.
- **Icon shape system**: the five container shapes are SVG masks with per-shape fill scaling so every shape reads at the same visual weight; the selection state uses a drop-shadow on a wrapper element (CSS filters composite before masks, so shadows on the masked element itself get clipped away). The same shapes are re-implemented as native paths in the WidgetKit views so widgets match the app pixel-for-pixel.
