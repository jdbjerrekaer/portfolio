## Recruiter takeaway

Sixteen interactions I designed and built into Adtraction's partner and brand platform. Every demo below runs the original React components and styles from the product, not a recording or a rebuild; only the data is fake. Together they show how I work as a design engineer: motion that explains a change of state, timing tuned per interaction, and the same care in the code as in the Figma file.

**All names and numbers in these demos are mock data.**

## What you are looking at

Adtraction is a Nordic affiliate network. Partners use the platform to find brands, apply to them and follow their earnings; brands use it to manage prices and partners. Over four years I designed and built much of its interface layer, including the shared UI toolkit these demos draw from.

The components were copied from the codebase with Adtraction's permission. Imports were rewired and the backend replaced with small fake agents; any other change is marked in the code as a portfolio edit. Hover, click, drag and use the keyboard: the states are all there.

**About the data:** every brand, channel, partner, person, URL and number shown here is invented for this page. None of it is real Adtraction data or describes a real customer, and any resemblance to a real company is coincidental.

## Data table

The table behind Insights, and the piece I am proudest of. I built it from the ground up: filter chips, a comparison mode that adds the previous period and a delta to every metric, a column chooser with pinning and drag-to-reorder whose rows enter in a stagger, and warning panels that slide in when a filter makes some metrics meaningless. Here it runs on the free edition of AG Grid, so a few enterprise-only features are off.

## Resizable side panel

Side panels open over the page, and you can drag the pill on their left edge to resize them. The pill follows the pointer up and down and grows while you drag. It stops at its minimum width; drag past the maximum and it stretches with resistance, then snaps back to the limit when you let go. A double-click resets the width.

## Tooltip timing

Tooltips across the platform share one timing rule: the first waits 600 ms, so it never pops up by accident, and once one has opened its neighbours open instantly for the next 1.4 s. You can feel it on the data table's column headers above, or by sliding down the collapsed sidebar's icons. Every tooltip also scales up, lifts and unblurs as it appears. The selection bar below overrides it with its own, shorter 300 ms delay.

## Description peek

Resting on a clamped brand description opens it a little, and holding near the bottom edge reveals more in steps. A short grace period and a cooldown stop it flickering when the cursor only passes over.

## List reorder

On the price page, segments re-sort as you edit them. Cards slide from their old position to the new one instead of jumping, so you never lose the one you just changed.

## Staggered entrances

Lists enter as a quick cascade rather than all at once. The cap matters as much as the delay: past a handful of items every card shares the last delay, so a long list never feels slow.

<StaggerDemo />

## Global search

The platform-wide search opens with ⌘K and stays quiet on fast searches: no loader at all under 400 ms, a thin progress bar after that, and a short "Still searching…" line only once a request passes 2.5 seconds. Results group pages above partners and channels, and the arrow keys, Enter and Escape drive the whole thing.

<GlobalSearchDemo />

## Load reveal

Brand pages load into a skeleton of the same layout. When the data arrives the skeleton blurs out, the content blurs in and the container eases to its real height, so nothing below it jumps.
