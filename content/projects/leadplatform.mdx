## The commercial problem

LeadPlatform is a product with a P&L, not a portfolio of microsites. Advertisers pay per qualified lead, so the business only works if three numbers stay in the right place.

| Lever | What it costs when it goes wrong |
| --- | --- |
| Conversion rate | Traffic is bought. A form converting at 2% instead of 3% raises the cost per lead by 50% on identical spend. |
| Lead quality | A lead the advertiser rejects, whether junk data, a duplicate, or no valid consent, is traffic already paid for and revenue never billed. |
| Cost to launch | If a campaign takes weeks of design and development, only large budgets are worth taking. Everything smaller gets declined. |

Design moves all three, and that is the job here. I have owned it since 2021, first at Adservice and now at Adtraction, across concept, UX, visual design and front-end.

## At a glance

- Role: Design Engineer, covering concept, UX, visual design, front-end and campaign tooling
- Scale: 60+ live campaigns across Denmark, Sweden, Norway and Finland
- Clients: UNICEF, Telia, DNA, Talkmore, Fortum, Plan BørneFonden, Lungeforeningen
- Business model: pay per campaign site, no annual licence, with traffic distributed through the Nordic affiliate network
- Context: 83% of Connect submissions came from a phone
- Result: Connect ran 30 April to 2 July 2025 and produced 4,133 lead submissions, 3,731 of them accepted (90.3%)

## Three levers

Conversion, lead quality and cost to launch. Two campaigns carry the first two, and the platform tooling carries the third.

## Lever 1: conversion, choosing the trade

Nobody wants to fill in a form. They want the thing on the other side of it. So the first decision on any brief is not visual. It is what the brand can offer that is worth someone's phone number, and the format follows from that.

| Format | What the visitor gets | Best when the advertiser has |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Quiz | Curiosity, and an answer about themselves | Knowledge worth sharing |
| Prize wheel | An immediate, visible reward | A prize but no story |
| Contest | A shot at something big | Budget for a prize that justifies the ask |
| Scratcher | Instant resolution, nothing to read | The lowest-attention traffic |
| Advent calendar | A reason to return tomorrow | A long campaign window to fill |

Getting this wrong is expensive before a single pixel is drawn. A charity with real expertise running a scratcher wastes the only asset it has.

## Lungeforeningen: making an unsellable category sellable

The Danish Lung Association needed people to self-check for early COPD symptoms. Commercially that is close to unadvertisable. Nobody clicks *you may be seriously ill*, and a symptom checklist reads as a diagnosis nobody asked for. Low click-through and low completion together mean the cost per lead never clears.

![Lungeforeningen campaign hero: a broken-down car in a field, its exhaust smoke spelling out HIV, HOST, HVÆS](/projects/leadplatform/lungeforeningen-hero.jpg)

The answer was a car. One broken down on a dirt road, steam pouring from the bonnet, and the steam spells the symptoms: *hiv, host, hvæs*, or gasp, cough, wheeze. The headline is **Pust nu!**, meaning *breathe now*, and the invitation is a question with a shrug in it: *do your lungs need a trip to the workshop?*

The metaphor is the commercial unlock, and it holds all the way through.

- The three questions have no wrong answers. Breathlessness, coughing and wheezing, each answered *no*, *recently*, or *for a long time*. Nothing can be failed, so nobody abandons out of anxiety. Underneath, each option carries a weight of 0, 1 or 3.
- The result is a car verdict rather than a diagnosis. Under 1: *roll the window down and drive on*. Under 3: *pull over and check if you need an oil change*. Above that: *hard shoulder, handbrake*. It is real triage, in language that lets someone take it seriously without panicking.
- Only the most serious verdict names the disease. The gentle results talk about cars. The severe one says asthma and COPD and tells you to call your doctor. The metaphor earns the right to break exactly where it matters.

![The Lungeforeningen sign-up card over the blurred hero, including the choice to answer for yourself or for a relative](/projects/leadplatform/lungeforeningen-form.jpg)

The detail with the clearest commercial effect is the smallest one. The form asks *who are you answering for, yourself or a relative?* A large share of the people worried about a cough are worried about a parent's. Without that option they either lie or leave. With it, the campaign's addressable audience roughly doubles and the leads stay accurate about who the person actually is. One radio group, and it changed who the campaign was for.

## Connect: one lead, three advertisers

Connect is co-sponsored. A single sign-up feeds three advertisers, Det Faglige Hus, Idényt and Grøn Elforsyning, under one consent. One sign-up can be billed to three advertisers instead of one, and that puts the entire commercial value of the campaign on one checkbox. If the consent is ambiguous, every lead collected is unusable and the campaign becomes a liability instead of an asset.

There is also no concept to hide behind. No metaphor, no illustration, just a general-interest health quiz where the only thing to design is the form. With 83% of submissions coming from a phone, the form is the landing page.

![LeadPlatform Connect on desktop: one white card centred over a green health photograph](/projects/leadplatform/connect-form-desktop.jpg)

Each decision, and what it is worth:

- One column, always. The only paired row is city and postcode, because they are one thought. Fewer eye jumps, fewer abandons.
- Placeholders do the work of labels, at full input height. Large tap targets, nothing above each field competing for space, and the whole form in a thumb's reach instead of a scroll.
- The offer sits directly above field one. *Win a 4,000 kr Netto gift card*, in italics. The reason to type is never scrolled off-screen, which is the most common and most expensive mistake in this format.
- One consent checkbox, full text, never behind a "read more". Naming all three companies in plain sight is slower to read and converts slightly worse. It is also the only version where the lead is legally sellable to all three. Hiding it behind an accordion is a dark pattern with extra steps, and it would risk the whole campaign to buy a fraction of a percent.
- A wheel picker for the birth date. Typing a date of birth is the worst field on any mobile form and a reliable abandon point. Three scrollable columns, iOS-style, answered by thumb with no keyboard.

![The same form on mobile: full-bleed, single column, large targets](/projects/leadplatform/connect-form-mobile.jpg)

After submitting, the quiz is a swipe-card deck, one statement per card, left for false and right for true. It exists to make the post-submit moment worth staying for, which is what makes the format re-runnable. Three details do the work. The first card nudges itself on entry so nobody needs instructions. Each answer flips to a fact whose display time is calculated from its length rather than a fixed timer, with tap-to-skip for faster readers. And the cards are height-normalised so the deck never jumps.

## Lever 2: lead quality

A lead the advertiser rejects costs more than a lead never collected, because the traffic was already bought. So validation is a design surface, not a back-end afterthought.

- Junk gets caught at the keyboard rather than at the invoice. Name fields reject gibberish and repeated-character patterns, alongside email, address, postcode and age checks. Errors surface inline, in the campaign's language, while the person is still on the page and willing to fix them.
- Duplicates and unauthorised traffic are filtered before a lead reaches the advertiser, so partners cannot resubmit the same person for another payout.
- Consent is a deliverable. Every lead carries a documented consent, a confirmation email and a self-service withdrawal flow. That is what makes a lead billable in four regulated markets rather than merely collected.
- Leads are forwarded into each advertiser's own system, so the client works in the CRM they already use and nothing sits waiting to be exported.

## Lever 3: cost to launch

This lever decides which campaigns the business can afford to say yes to. All of it moves work off the critical path.

- The campaign builder is aimed at campaign managers, not developers. An in-product admin panel edits headlines, prize, offer copy, which fields appear, quiz questions and answers, exit pages and language across Danish, Swedish, Norwegian and Finnish, with live preview. Changing an offer mid-flight is a campaign-manager task rather than a developer ticket, which means underperforming campaigns get fixed instead of finished.
- Campaigns are configuration. Format, fields, copy and prize are settings, so a new campaign is a config change rather than a codebase.
- One command scaffolds a new branded campaign from a template, with the brand applied, routing wired and settings written. Brief to live in hours.
- A shared, themed component library covers inputs, selects, checkboxes, date and birthdate pickers and buttons, with a live preview page. Every campaign inherits the conversion work already done on the last one, and only the brand layer changes.
- Banners, prize tiles and share images render from templates instead of being cut by hand, so ads never drift from the page they point at.

The compounding effect matters more than any single piece. A conversion improvement designed once is inherited by all 60 campaigns, and the fixed cost per campaign is low enough that small and mid-size budgets become profitable inventory instead of declined briefs.

## Impact

- 60+ live branded campaigns across four Nordic markets, from one templated platform
- Connect, 30 April to 2 July 2025: 4,133 lead submissions across Idényt, Det Faglige Hus and Grøn Elforsyning, 3,731 accepted (90.3%)
- 23.3% accepted submissions per affiliate click on Connect (3,731 against 16,034 tracked clicks; a submission is counted once per sponsor, so this is not a per-person rate)
- Rejections were concentrated in two places: a generic error from one sponsor's endpoint (199) and an age rule at another (birth date must be under 65, 42 cases), which is the kind of thing validation at the keyboard removes
- Brief to live in hours, with no per-campaign design or build cycle
- Design, traffic and integration handled by one partner, priced per site with no annual licence

Lead generation is usually designed as an obstacle standing between someone and a prize. It performs better as an exchange, and the numbers above are what that difference is worth.
