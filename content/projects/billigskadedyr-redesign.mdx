## The situation: one page to sell a service

Daniel Nemborg runs a pest-control company that covers Jylland and Fyn, CVR 40306633. For the professional side of the business, the part where a technician comes to your house, his website was one page on billigskadedyrprof.dk. A full-screen photograph of a spider. A contact form floating on it, "Indtast dine oplysninger og få et tilbud på få minutter". A menu of five links that all stay on that one page. Below the form, long stretches of empty, unstyled sections.

The reader is a Danish homeowner, usually on a phone, often in the room with the problem, deciding whether to let a stranger into their house. The business also runs a DIY webshop on a separate domain; that shop is not this project and it stays where it is. This case study is the professional site, which has existed in three versions: the one page, the audit and concept I built from it, and the pest wiki the concept grew into. Before touching anything I archived the whole presence, every route at desktop and phone widths plus Wayback snapshots, on 29 August 2026.

## What was there, and why it did not work

**Credit first.** The shop half of the business works. Guest checkout, broad payment coverage, and genuinely fast: I measured a 156 ms largest contentful paint on mobile, which killed the speed argument I had planned to lead with. A statistic that does not describe the site in front of you is decoration, so it is out. The professional page had no such defence.

### The page could not sell the service

One page, one form. The "Vælg service" dropdown listed 13 pests plus "Andet", so the visitor reads all 14 options before being allowed to submit. There was no price anywhere, no explanation of what happens after you send the form, and no social proof. Placeholder Lorem ipsum sat in production in the wasp section, visible to every visitor. "Stankelben", a harmless insect, was listed as a service. And below the fold the page simply stopped: sections rendered as empty blocks on a dark background.

This matters more here than in most categories, because of what the visitor is deciding. In [Stanford's web credibility research](https://advocacy.consumerreports.org/research/how-do-people-evaluate-a-web-sites-credibility/), 2,684 people judging real sites named the look of the site as the most frequent factor in trusting or distrusting it, in 46.1% of comments, ahead of the content. A page that ships placeholder text is telling every visitor that nobody has looked at it lately, and a visitor extends that reading to the company and to the person who would show up at their door.

### The business's web presence read as unmaintained, so I measured it

Easy to assert, easy to dismiss as taste. So I measured the substantial half, the shop homepage: 8 distinct corner radii in use, 9 distinct text sizes, 4 font families on one page, 136 inline style attributes, and a most common body text size on mobile of 12px. Here is what those radii look like as components:

![Four components from the live homepage, each captioned with its own computed border-radius: a search form wrapper at 28.5px, a round button at 50%, the search input at 25px, and a link button at 9px.](/images/billigskadedyr/evidence-radii.png)

The first two tiles are the same search field: a wrapper rounded to 28.5px with the input inside it rounded to 25px. Nobody chooses three and a half pixels of difference on purpose. That is the residue of a site assembled rather than designed, and it is what a visitor feels without being able to name it.

### The trust assets sat one level away

The business demonstrably had reviews: 3.8 out of 5 on Trustpilot, 4.6 on e-mærket with 110 verified reviews. None of it was on the professional page. The questions that decide this sale, do you cover my address, are you allowed to do this, what happens if it goes wrong, were answered in the FAQ, inside collapsed accordions, and the answer under "Er der garanti på jeres arbejde?" was wired to the wrong paragraph entirely. Meanwhile the shop asked for a card number with prices formatted `1,199.00kr.` and delivery cost that never appeared, and [Baymard's abandonment research](https://baymard.com/blog/cart-abandonment-rate-reasons) puts cost revealed late at the top of why people leave. Different site, same lesson: tell people the full price before you ask for their details.

## What I did

I worked from Baymard for pricing and forms, [NN/g's research on how people read](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/how-little-do-users-read/) for the copy, the Laws of UX for interface decisions, with WCAG 2.2 AA as the floor.

**Lead with the price, not the form.** The old page's only next step was "fill in your details and get a quote". The rebuild answers the price question on the first screen: pick your pest, see one number. The estimator originally returned a range and I killed it, because a range makes the reader do arithmetic and stays uncertain, which is the old page's problem restated politely. The refinements sit behind one control with defaults that are true, the area presets from the actual regional averages (Danmarks Statistik, floor area over dwelling counts for Fyn and the five Jylland regions): 79 m² for a flat, 140 m² for a house. A default is an answer the page gives on the visitor's behalf, and a preset never overwrites a number the visitor set themselves. One more decision worth naming: the included-list derives from the selections. Rats list rodent-proofing, wasps list nest removal, a house with several buildings hides the area question because the discount rules make it moot. A list that never changes is decoration; a list that changes is feedback.

![The estimator section on desktop: pest, area, property type and severity on the left, one concrete price with the derived included-list on the right](/images/billigskadedyr/after-estimator.jpg)

**Navigate by pest, not by service vocabulary.** Nobody wakes up wanting a "Vælg service" dropdown. They wake up because they heard something in the wall. The rebuild names the animal, rats, ants, wasps, bed bugs, silverfish, and lets the visitor recognise what they have seen instead of recalling what the trade calls it.

**Show the weaker review score.** Both ratings sit on the page with their real counts and a link out, the 3.8 as honestly as the 4.6. Hiding the lower one is the intuitive move and the wrong one, because a visitor who finds it independently now discounts everything else on the page.

**State the business model in plain words.** The owner gives no guarantee and no paperwork, only a receipt, and that is the reason he is cheaper: competitors price free re-visits into every job and lose money on them. The site says this out loud, "ingen garanti, intet papir", as the sales argument rather than a hidden caveat. His pricing rules live in one file in the code, and a check script fails the build if they drift: ants never above 1.200 kr in Herning, Holstebro and Viborg, spiders always sold as a two-visit package floored at 3.000 kr, with an optional in-season bonus the estimator asks about instead of assuming.

**Grow the page into the reference the business never had.** The site did not stay a landing page. It is now 22 pest categories covering what a Danish house or garden is likely to meet, 76 species articles for the searches someone runs at midnight, "fugleloppe", "pelsklanner", "vingetæge", across 232 prerendered pages. Every article answers the identification question in its first screen and ends in the same two actions. The wiki is the top of the funnel, the service pages are the bottom, and every article links down.

Two rules from this build earned their place. Every species drawing carries its source page, license and edits in a SOURCES.md file in the repository, so the asset trail a client would otherwise ask a stock vendor for is a text file. And a drawing shows for all of a card's species or none: a drawing beside a placeholder icon reads as the stock-photo laziness this audit criticises. When no public-domain engraving of the hen flea could be found, the third flea drawing was made as ink from a 1962 CDC photomicrograph before the page shipped any of them.

**Prerender every page, so no content depends on JavaScript.** The old shop's article URLs returned twelve identical empty shells, which I found by reading the raw documents rather than the rendered page. The build now runs every page through a headless browser and writes the result back into its own HTML. A cold arrival from a search result paints content on the first frame, and a crawler gets the full text.

**Design the phone as its own surface.** Popovers become bottom sheets pinned to the visual viewport with a grabber and safe-area insets. A price chip, pest and price and call to action, rides over the phone's own bar while the visitor adjusts the estimator, because on a page that long the price is otherwise out of view exactly when it changes. Hover states exist only under a hover-capable pointer, so touch never hangs in a state it cannot reach.

**Hold the build to the same standard as the audit.** I ran my own measurements over the concept and it failed too: the lime accent sat at 1.79:1 on white, across 8 elements. The fix was one token taken dark enough to read, and the re-run showed 0 failing elements. Touch targets stay at 44px, focus is always visible, and reduced motion turns every animation off. The checks guard the routes as well: one fails the build if any page lacks its prerendered shell, after a real incident where a missing shell served a directory listing that looked blank to crawlers and people alike. Another enforces a one-way door: the shop may link to the professional site, the professional site never links back. The professional site argues for the technician, and it does not open a door back into the catalogue.

## Working with the owner

The owner is not a web person, and the decisions that mattered were his. His written feedback was lost once to a mistimed stop, and one review round went out without it; the next round opened with his message recovered verbatim, and the corrections in it were the ones that stuck. He picked the current visual theme himself on 23 October, over three theme experiments of mine that sit in the repository as abandoned files, and the design documentation now records the no-guarantee positioning in one line so no future edit quietly reintroduces a promise the business does not make. When his answers contradicted the page, the page lost: the concept claimed coverage of "i hele Aarhus" while his own FAQ said he covers all of Jylland and Fyn, turning away every qualifying homeowner in Aalborg and Esbjerg on behalf of a business that would happily have served them.

## Status

The site runs as a fully prerendered static build, 232 pages, with the route, image, pricing and one-way checks green. It is ready to move from the preview server to the professional domain once the DNS moves. The before-state is archived, so the comparison stays checkable after the old page is gone.
