## What this is

Adservice Influencer was the landing page for Adservice's influencer network, from my first months as the company's lead designer. The Figma file has three frames: desktop at 1440px, iPad at 768px and iPhone at 320px.

It is worth showing now for a reason I did not see coming. Adtraction bought Adservice in early 2024, and today I design [Iriz](../iriz/), Adtraction's app for influencers. Different company name, different medium, same question: how do you get a brand and a creator to the same place when they want opposite things from it?

## Two visitors, one page

A brand wants reach it can measure. An influencer wants a sponsor that fits their feed and pays. The page does not try to talk to both at once. It asks who you are and splits there.

| Where | Brand | Influencer |
|---|---|---|
| Hero promise | "Connect with your target audience in a non-intrusive way through influencers" | "Advertise brands your audience love and earn money in the process" |
| Hero button | I'm a brand | I'm an influencer |
| Contact pitch | "Reach customers in a non-intrusive way by using our network of influencers." | "Keep your work's integrity intact by finding a matching brand to sponsor you." |
| Third form field | Brand | Profile link |
| Submit | Get in Contact | Apply as Influencer |

The two forms share Name and Email and differ by one field. That field is the whole difference between the two relationships: a brand tells us who it is, an influencer shows us their work.

## The sections

![Two phones next to the full service influencer network pitch](/projects/adservice-influencer/network.jpg)

The pitch sits on a green band cut at an angle, with two phones showing the camera screens influencers actually work in. Under it, the brand logo strip carries the social proof.

![Four audience categories as a photo grid](/projects/adservice-influencer/audiences.jpg)

Audiences are sold as categories, not follower counts: Travel, Fitness, Health and Lifestyle, Fashion. Two of the four tiles still carry placeholder copy in the file.

![A publisher dashboard shown on a laptop](/projects/adservice-influencer/interface.jpg)

For the brand side, the page borrows Adservice's own reporting interface: revenue for this month, this week, yesterday and today, conversions arriving by country, and the best campaigns of the day. The point of the section is that influencer spend gets tracked like any other channel.

![Hotels.com story mock and campaign statistics](/projects/adservice-influencer/case-hotels.jpg)

The case section shows what a campaign looks like from the audience's side: an Instagram story with a poll sticker, framed by the brand. The +76% and 39% next to it are layout figures in the comp, not results I can attribute to a real campaign.

![Contact forms for brands and influencers](/projects/adservice-influencer/contact.jpg)

## Down to 320px

![The contact section stacked on a phone](/projects/adservice-influencer/mobile-contact.jpg)

On iPad the page keeps its two columns and shrinks the type. On a 320px phone the split becomes a sequence: hero buttons stack, the category grid becomes a column, and the two contact forms stack with a rule between them, brand first.

## Then and now

| | Adservice Influencer (2022) | Iriz (2024) |
|---|---|---|
| Medium | Responsive website | iOS and Android app |
| Who it talks to | Brands and influencers, split on one page | Influencers only |
| Getting in | Apply through a form, reviewed by the team | Invite code, then connect a social channel |
| What the influencer sees first | A pitch and a form | Brands and their per-click rate |
| Proof of earnings | A reporting dashboard shown on a laptop | Earnings on every link card |

The 2022 page sells the network and hands over to a sales conversation. Iriz skips the conversation: the network is already in the app, and the earnings are on the card. Reading the two side by side, most of what changed is how much of the relationship moved from people into the product.
