## What this is

Iriz is Adtraction's affiliate platform reduced to what an influencer actually does with it. The desktop partner platform is built for publishers who run reports. An influencer runs a phone, posts a link, and wants to know two things: which brands pay per click, and what did my last link make.

So the app has three tabs. Discover, Link, Account. Everything else was cut.

The screens below are Figma, not screenshots. The product is real and sits behind a login, so the design files are the honest way to show it.

## Positioning first

Before any screen, a colleague and I mapped the field. Six platforms an influencer in the Nordics might already be using, each pulled apart on a board: who it is for, how a link gets made, what it pays on, which markets it is in.

| Platform | Pays on | Aimed at | What we noted |
|---|---|---|---|
| Metapic (Tradedoubler) | Per click | Micro to macro, Nordics | Link flow is open the store in a new window, copy the URL, paste it into a converter. Just launched Zezam, a link-in-bio profile |
| LTK Creator | Commission | Mid to top tier, fashion and beauty | Invite-gated. The influencer app everyone else is compared to |
| Klarna Creator | Per sale | Micro to macro, Klarna merchants | Strong on merchant reach, tied to Klarna's checkout |
| Stylink | Per click | Nano and micro | Fast small payouts, low barrier. Not in Norway |
| Make Influence | Performance | Micro to mid tier, Denmark | Brand-led campaigns, matchmaking |
| Upfluence | Brand tool | Brands, not creators | No open marketplace; creators do not sign up themselves |

Two of those are per-click and both are the closest to Iriz. Metapic's link flow was the most useful finding on the board: three steps across two windows for the one thing the product exists to do.

![Two positioning maps from the board. Design aesthetics: muted to bright, playful to serious. Technical: platform to app, open to exclusive. Iriz is placed muted and serious, and app and exclusive.](/projects/iriz/positioning-maps.jpg)

Then two maps. On design aesthetics, the field clusters playful and bright (Upfluence, Klarna, Make Influence). Iriz was placed muted and serious, next to LTK, which is where the influencers in our personas already live: neutral palettes, editorial photography, no confetti. On the technical map, most of the field is a web platform open to anyone. Iriz was placed as an app, and exclusive. That second placement drove more decisions than the first.

**Exclusive on purpose.** The brief on the board says it directly: "the feeling of being very exclusive, only available for a selective few, relying on word of mouth and invite only." That is why the app has no public sign-up screen in these files, why brands appear as stories rather than as a catalogue, and why the visual register is closer to a fashion magazine than to a dashboard.

![The brainstorm brief from the board: context, features, problems the app will solve, and other values](/projects/iriz/brief.jpg)

**A skin, not a new platform.** The other line that mattered: "a slim version of the Adtraction UI, with the relevant features for influencers extracted." Iriz runs on the same network, the same brands, the same tracking. The design job was subtraction: decide which five things an influencer needs and make everything else disappear.

We also drafted six personas to argue with (a luxury aesthetic influencer, a tech trendsetter, a wellness creator, a Gen Z chaos account, a mom-fluencer, a trendy beauty creator). They were written with an AI assistant and used as a checklist for taste, not as research. Where they earned their keep was the aesthetics map: five of the six describe their look as minimal, soft or neutral, which settled the muted end of the axis.

## Discover

![Discover feed: stories, an editorial card, recently viewed, deals, a Fashion row, and Most linked from](/projects/iriz/home.jpg)

The feed borrows the grammar influencers already live in. Brands appear as stories at the top, the same circle-and-ring pattern as Instagram, so a new brand reads as new content rather than as a database entry. Under it, an editorial card with a headline over a photograph, because the platform's own content ("10 Instagram friendly meals we love right now") is what gets someone to open the app when nothing is earning.

Then the rows: recently viewed, deals right now, a category row, and "Most linked from". Each row is a reason to scroll, not a filter.

**The rate is the label.** Every brand card carries one number under the logo, in a small sparkle-tagged line: *0.50 kr/click*. Not commission percentages, not EPC, not tiers. The whole product is per-click pricing, so the price per click is the only number an influencer needs to compare two brands, and it sits in the same place on every card.

**Deals are chips with a deadline.** "24% in sunscreen" with a red *Last chance* tag, "3 for 2 on socks" with a yellow *5 days left*. Urgency is a colour, not a sentence.

**Products, not just brands.** The "Most linked from" row shows a brand with four product thumbnails under it. A brand is abstract; a pair of sandals is something you can post tonight.

## Search

![Search before typing: new-brand banner, deals, loved brands, category grid](/projects/iriz/search-start.jpg)

Search is a page before it is a field. Opening the tab shows a new-brand banner, current deals, the brands you have hearted, and a category grid of photographs with a single word on each. Most searches on a discovery app are browsing, so the empty state does the browsing for you.

![Fashion category: promos on top, then a grid of brand cards](/projects/iriz/search-category.jpg)

Inside a category, promos come first as small list cards, then a two-column brand grid. The same card, the same rate line, the same position, whether the brand was reached from the feed, a category or a search result.

![Brands you love: filter chips with counts](/projects/iriz/search-loved.jpg)

"Brands you love" gets its own view with chips that carry counts: *All 27, Fashion 2, Travel 2, Food 2*. The count tells you whether the filter is worth tapping before you tap it. The chip itself was redesigned for this app and went back into the Iriz design system.

![Searching for Hello: one matching brand](/projects/iriz/search-typing.jpg)

Typing collapses everything to results, with the search field pushed down to sit just above the keyboard where the thumb already is. One result is shown as one card, not a list row, so the rate stays visible.

## Create link

This is the tab the product exists for.

![Create link: empty paste field over a soft photographic background](/projects/iriz/link-input.jpg)

**One field, one instruction.** The screen is a photograph, a paste field, and the line "Insert a product to create your link". No brand picker, no campaign selector, no link type. The influencer has already found the product on the brand's site; the app's job is to turn that URL into a tracked one.

![A bubbleroom.se product URL pasted into the field](/projects/iriz/link-apply.jpg)

**Paste, and the brand is detected.** The URL identifies the brand and the product. If the brand is not on the platform, the app says so in one line rather than failing quietly. If the link is a homepage rather than a product, it says that too. Those error states were designed as first-class screens, because a paste that goes wrong is the moment someone decides whether to try again.

![Link created: My links grouped into folders and channel tabs with earnings per link](/projects/iriz/link-product.jpg)

**The link lands in a folder and a channel.** "My links" is organised two ways at once: folders the influencer names (*Skincare favs*, *Must haves*) and the channel the link is for (*Instagram*, *Tiktok*, *Pinterest*). Both are chips with counts. A link is copied with the button on the right, and the earnings sit on the card: *15 498 kr* on one, *0.00 kr/click* in red on another with a *Low audience engagement* warning above it. The dashboard is the list itself. There is no separate reporting screen to learn.

**The status dot.** Each product thumbnail carries a small dot, green or red, in its corner. It is the smallest possible way to say "this one is working" across a list of twenty links.

## What I would defend

- Showing the per-click rate on every card in the same spot. It turns the whole app into a price comparison, which is what the product is.
- The paste-first link flow. Every step removed from "found a product" to "have a link" is a step an influencer would otherwise do in a browser.
- Errors as designed screens. Unsupported brand, broken link, homepage instead of product, general failure: four states, each with one line of copy.

## What is not here

No usage numbers. The app is a real product behind a login, but I do not have adoption or earnings data I can cite, and a made-up figure would be worse than none. The screens are the case.
